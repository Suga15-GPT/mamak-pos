## Findings, most severe first

### F1. Following the first-update steps can cut a device off from the server (Medium; not new: main's worker causes it, and last round's steps reached it too)
- What goes wrong:
  - Until a device has finished its first update, main's service worker answers its pages' requests. It sends the
    live stream `/api/stream` through `networkFirst`, which also copies the answer into its cache
    (`cache.put(request, res.clone())`, public/sw.js:72-75 on main).
    - A stream never ends, and the server keeps it open with a ping every 25 s (src/routes/stream.js:33).
    - So the connection stays open after its tab is closed. 7f2c50b's worker leaves the stream alone, and with it
      the connection is let go.
  - The tills reach the PC over plain HTTP (`http://<PC address>:3000`), and Chromium opens at most six connections
    to one server. Once six are held by such streams, nothing more from that device reaches the server.
  - The new steps (docs/RUNBOOK.md:376-384) close every POS tab and open the POS again, twice, then "once more"
    while the version line is missing. Each tab closed while main's worker is in charge can keep one connection.
    - If the person opens all of the device's tabs again in each step, a device with three tabs (the POS, the
      Kitchen screen, Sales) uses up the six in the first step, before the new version arrives. It never gets it.
    - If one POS tab is left open somewhere, main's worker stays in charge, and each "once more" holds one more
      connection.
- Reproduction (`t7_conn.js`):
  - Setup: main → 7f2c50b on one port and schema, Malaysia time; one device is one Chromium profile; no proxy (the
    browser talks to the server directly); connections counted with `netstat`.
  - The steps exactly: two close-and-opens, then "once more" up to 3 times.

  | Device | Opened again in each step | Pace (closed / looking) | Result, 2 runs each |

  | 1 POS tab | the POS | 0.3 s / 0.8 s | on 7f2c50b after 3 |

  | 2 tabs (POS, Kitchen) | both | 1 s / 2 s, 2 s / 3 s, 2 s / 5 s | on 7f2c50b after 2 (6 of 6) |
  | 2 tabs | both | 0.3 s / 0.8 s | still on main after 5; no cards drawn; 6 connections held |
  | 3 tabs (POS, Kitchen, Sales) | all three | 2 s / 5 s | still on main after 5; no cards from the 2nd step; 6 held |
  | 3 tabs | only the POS (the others after the check) | 2 s / 5 s | on 7f2c50b after 2 |

  - A POS tab left open (the Kitchen tab), the POS closed and opened as the steps say: the new version waited

  - On a stuck device, a GET and an order POST sent from the page got no answer in 10 s.

    - e8e7222's steps (the Kitchen tab left open, the POS closed and opened 5 times): still on main, and no cards

- What it costs the shop:
  - On the update day, a till that cannot load bills, send orders or take payment until the app is restarted.
  - The RUNBOOK's own way out ("close every POS tab once more") cannot help, and each round holds one more
    connection.
  - No money is recorded wrongly; the device simply stops reaching the server.
- Suggested fix:
  - In docs/RUNBOOK.md:
    - Steps 2–3: "then open one POS tab again; open the Kitchen screen and other screens only after step 4 passes".
    - Step 4: "If the version line is still missing after the second time, stop closing and opening. Restart the
      app on the server, then do steps 2–4 once."
  - Optionally, in code: the new server could end each live stream after a few minutes. main's page reconnects and
    asks for what it missed (`?since=`), and the server ending the stream frees what main's worker holds, even when
    the steps go wrong.

## Method

- Database and setup:
  - Only the throwaway Postgres at localhost:5434. Every script refuses any other address.
  - The review folder was fast-forwarded to 7f2c50b and ran it.
  - Controls on e8e7222, main (d947fab) and d5a9b4b came from `git archive` in my scratch folder.
  - Every schema came fresh from its tree's migrations; 7f2c50b's include 022.
  - Gemini was a local stand-in.
- Time zones: everything in Malaysia time, as docker-compose runs the app. The expense races, E7, the decimals, the
  nits and the Sales tiles ran again in UTC.
- Real taps: Chromium mouse clicks at one point, with gaps of 0–1000 ms, at 1280×800, 800×1280 and 390×844.
- Builds: each build ran as its own server process on one port and one schema, with service workers on, each device
  in its own Chromium profile. Chromium 151 (Playwright 1.62.1).
- Connections:
  - Counted with `netstat` with no proxy, as in the table in F1.
  - An earlier set of runs through a counting proxy got stuck more often than the same runs without it. The likely
    cause is that the proxy did not end old streams when the server restarted. Those runs are not used for the
    update case.
  - They are used only for main on its own, where there is no restart and the no-proxy runs agree.
- Races: 40 runs each (200 for E7), every ordering of the two sides.
- Scripts re-run from earlier reports: `exp_checks.js`, `edge_checks.js`, `gemini_check.js`, `root_check.js`,
  `prev_check.js`, `upgrade_021.js`, `explorer_z2.js`, `split_fuzz.js`, `n3_races.js`, `clear_races.js`,
